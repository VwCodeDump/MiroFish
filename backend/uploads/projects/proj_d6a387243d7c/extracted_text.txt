# Seed Data: Educational Tool Needs of Portugal-Based Students

**Purpose:** Seed document for a Mirofish simulation of 10,000 Portugal-based students. Goal: find out which educational tools and features students need, what they would use and pay for, and what Magic Book Technologies should build.

**Language / market note:** All agents are in Portugal and use European Portuguese (PT-PT), not Brazilian Portuguese. Use Portuguese school terms (ano, ciclo, exames nacionais, explicações, explicador, média, acesso ao ensino superior).

---

## 1. Verified market context (grounded in public sources)

**Private tutoring (explicações) is a large, exam-driven market**
- About 20% of students aged 6–18 in Portugal attend explicações outside school, roughly 200,000 students. The market moves over €30M per month. Source: Fundação "la Caixa" Observatório Social study, "O mercado das explicações na Península Ibérica".
- Incidence peaks in secondary school: about 1 in 3 students (33%). Exam preparation is a main motivation. Weaker students and students who have failed a year are more likely to take explicações. Same study.
- Wealthier families spend about 28% more on explicações than financially struggling families, so access is unequal. Same study.
- A platform called Fixando reports a 65% rise in demand for national-exam tutoring in 2024/25, with over 53% of those students attending more than once a week (reported by Empreendedor.com). Treat as a single-platform indicator, not a national statistic.

**National exams drive student behaviour**
- Three national exams remain mandatory for higher-education access. Exams now weigh 25% of the final subject grade (previously 30%). Source: RTP News.
- Digital national exams are in a pilot phase; students are being given practice platforms ahead of them. Source: DNoticias.

**AI is already part of how students study**
- PISA 2025 (OECD) indicates almost half of Portuguese students already use AI to study, and most say they check the answers AI gives them. Source: RFM, reporting PISA 2025 data.

**Digital equipment is widespread but learning impact is unproven**
- The Digital School programme distributed 362,867 kits to students and 83,662 to teachers. An evaluation (Portugal 2020) found that using digital tools in class does not by itself significantly improve student success, and that changing teaching methods remains uncommon.
- A survey of 2,041 Portuguese primary and secondary students (Cid, Coppi, Fialho, Monteiro, Leite) found the main perceived advantage of digital platforms is easy access to educational information. The main difficulties are platform malfunction and internet access. Younger students mostly use platforms to access content; older students use them to communicate and create content.

**Teachers' digital skills are intermediate**
- A DigCompEdu study of 170,000+ teachers in Spain and Portugal (RIED, 2026) found most at intermediate level (B1–B2). Their weakest area is developing students' own digital competence (critical literacy, online safety).

## 2. Population design for 10,000 agents

> The percentages below are **design assumptions** for the simulation, not official statistics. Adjust to match INE / DGEEC / PORDATA figures if you want closer realism.

**Education level**
| Segment | Share |
|---|---|
| 2.º ciclo (5.º–6.º ano) | 12% |
| 3.º ciclo (7.º–9.º ano) | 25% |
| Secundário, científico-humanístico (10.º–12.º) | 28% |
| Secundário, cursos profissionais | 8% |
| Ensino superior, licenciatura | 20% |
| Ensino superior, mestrado | 7% |

For 2.º/3.º ciclo agents, include a parent/guardian voice (encarregado de educação) on purchase decisions.

**Other variables to vary across agents**
- Region: Lisboa/AML, Norte (Porto), Centro, Alentejo, Algarve, Açores, Madeira; urban vs. rural/interior
- Household income: low / middle / high (affects ability to pay for explicações and tools)
- Device and connectivity: shared device vs. own laptop/tablet; reliable vs. patchy internet
- Academic profile: struggling / average / high achiever / exam-anxious
- Explicações: currently attends / used to / never; weekly spend
- AI attitude: heavy user / occasional / sceptical / avoids it (school rules, cheating worries)
- Special needs: dyslexia, ADHD, other learning support; Portuguese as second language (migrant students, including Brazilian, Ukrainian, PALOP, Chinese)
- Habits: paper notebooks vs. fully digital; phone-first vs. laptop-first
- Willingness to pay: free only / small one-off / monthly subscription / family-paid

**Include sceptics deliberately.** Current feedback on our products is only positive, so at least 25–30% of agents should be neutral, critical or unwilling to pay.

## 3. Candidate tool concepts to test (hypotheses, not findings)

Agents should react to, rank, and suggest changes to these, and propose their own.

1. **National exam prep studio.** Past-exam practice, step-by-step solutions, exam-style marking, mock digital exams in the pilot format.
2. **AI tutor in PT-PT.** Explains concepts at the student's level, grounded in the Portuguese curriculum, shows reasoning, cites the source for its answers.
3. **Affordable explicações alternative.** AI tutoring plus optional human explicador marketplace, priced below typical private tutoring.
4. **Notebook scan to study content** (MagicBook-style). Photograph handwritten or printed notes, get summaries, flashcards, quizzes.
5. **Homework helper.** Guides the student through steps instead of giving answers.
6. **Adaptive practice and weak-spot finder.** Diagnoses gaps by subject and topic, builds a practice plan.
7. **Spaced-repetition flashcards and memorisation tools.**
8. **Study planner and exam-countdown coach.** Includes focus timers and workload balancing.
9. **Média and university-access calculator.** Simulates internal grade + exam weights, shows achievable courses and entry grades.
10. **Exam-anxiety and wellbeing support.** Calm-down routines, study-stress check-ins.
11. **Accessibility pack.** Dyslexia-friendly text, read-aloud, speech-to-text, simplified explanations.
12. **Lecture/class summariser** (for higher education). Audio-to-notes, slide summaries.
13. **Writing and thesis assistant** (for higher education). Structure, referencing, plagiarism-safe feedback.
14. **Peer study rooms.** Group sessions, shared notes, study streaks.
15. **Parent/guardian dashboard.** Progress view, with privacy limits for older students.
16. **Portuguese-language support** for students who are new to the language.

---

## 4. Questions every agent must answer

1. What are your top 3 study struggles right now (by subject and by task type)?
2. Which tools do you use today (school platforms, YouTube, ChatGPT or other AI, explicações, apps, textbooks) and what do you like or dislike about each?
3. Rate each candidate concept (Section 3): need (1–5), would you use it weekly (yes/no), and what is missing.
4. Which single tool would you adopt first, and why?
5. What would make you stop using it or distrust it (wrong answers, cheating risk, privacy, ads, cost, school disapproval)?
6. How much would you or your family pay per month: €0, up to €5, €5–10, €10–20, over €20? Compared with what you currently spend on explicações?
7. Which features must be free?
8. What device and connectivity constraints would affect use?
9. How do you want AI to behave (give answers vs. coach; strictness about honesty; tone)?
10. What would you want the tool to do that nothing today does?

## 5. Required output report

Ask Mirofish to produce:

**Quantitative**
- Ranked list of the 16 concepts by need score and weekly-use intent, overall and by segment (level, region, income, AI attitude)
- Willingness-to-pay distribution and price-sensitivity curve by segment
- Adoption blockers ranked by frequency
- Top 10 requested features outside our list

**Qualitative**
- Student personas (5–8) with needs, objections and trigger moments (exam season, failed test, etc.)
- Concept-by-concept feedback: what to build, what to change, what to drop
- Recommended MVP (3–5 features) and a phased roadmap
- Positioning and messaging that resonates in Portugal (including school and parent trust)
- Risks: cheating perception, GDPR and minors' data, school-policy conflicts, AI answer quality
- Differences between segments that change the product (e.g. 9.º ano vs. 12.º ano vs. university)

---

## 6. Simulation guardrails

- Agents speak and think as realistic Portuguese students; not all are tech-enthusiastic and not all are positive.
- Keep agent responses consistent with their profile (income, region, grades, devices).
- Do not assume students will pay just because they like a tool; separate "nice to have" from "would pay for it."
- Where agents disagree, preserve the disagreement in the report rather than averaging it away.
- Flag results as simulated opinion, to be validated with a real student survey or interviews (suggest 30–50 interviews in Lisboa, Porto, one interior region).

---

## 7. Known gaps (fill before or after the run)

- Official student counts per level and region (INE / DGEEC / PORDATA) to calibrate agent shares
- Actual tutoring prices per hour by subject and level
- Competing products' pricing in Portugal (school-linked platforms, tutoring marketplaces, general AI chat apps)
- School and ministry policy on AI use in homework and exams (changes quickly)
- Real student interview data to validate simulated outputs

Critical quality observation
In one screenshot, option A is highlighted in green and marked with a check, but the panel below
displays “Incorrect” while also stating “Almost, the correct answer is A”. This combination is
contradictory. It should be investigated as a possible grading-logic error, a mismatch between the
selected answer and the answer key, or an incorrect visual feedback state. Testing the consistency
between the selected option, correct answer, colour/icon, and explanatory text is a priority.

18. Cross-functional acceptance criteria
The student can complete onboarding and later change the preferences that are editable.
The student can create and organise subjects and link chapters to them.
A captured page is processed, reviewed, and saved without losing the original image.
The reviewed text is used as the source for all subsequent generations.
All four post-scan actions work and retain a reference to the source page.

Flashcards and quizzes display accurate progress and content consistent with the source.
Quiz feedback always matches the selected answer and the answer key.
The mind map maintains a clear and coherent hierarchy.
AiA uses the correct context and does not mix subjects or users.
The essay checker provides an explainable assessment, and the semantic checker explains every
highlighted passage.
Learning path states correctly reﬂect completion, the next activity, and locked stages.
The classes area displays content only to authorised users.

20. Conclusion
The screenshots reveal a product with an integrated value proposition: starting with content actually
created by the student, digitising it, organising it, and turning it into AI-supported learning experiences.
The differentiating element is not merely the generation of quizzes or summaries, but the connection
between the original page, the academic context, the AiA assistant, and practice and assessment
activities.
To turn this experience into a development-ready speciﬁcation, the next steps are to formalise the
business rules, states and permissions, data contracts, AI quality criteria, and the teacher-area
workﬂows that have not yet been demonstrated. The contradictory quiz feedback issue requires
immediate validation.
