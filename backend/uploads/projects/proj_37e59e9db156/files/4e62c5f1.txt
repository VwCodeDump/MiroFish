## Overview

Evaluating text-to-video models effectively demands a two-pronged strategy that integrates both automatic quantitative metrics and nuanced human assessments. Automatic metrics provide objective data points for various aspects of video generation. For instance, visual fidelity is measured by metrics like FID and LPIPS, which scrutinize individual frame quality, while FVD gauges the temporal realism and naturalness of motion. Text-video alignment, crucial for ensuring the generated content matches the input prompt, is quantified using CLIPScore or CLIP-Cosine. Furthermore, for models generating dialogue, ASR-based lip-sync scores and audio-visual sync metrics are vital for confirming the synchronization between sound and visuals. Action recognition consistency, leveraging datasets such as UCF101 and Kinetics, helps assess how consistently actions are depicted across frames, with custom datasets facilitating character and identity specific tests.

Despite the utility of automatic metrics, human evaluations remain indispensable for capturing the subjective aspects of perceived quality that quantitative measures often miss. Methods like pairwise A/B tests and Likert ratings allow for the assessment of perceptual realism, narrative coherence, character consistency, and the overall quality of audio synchronization. To ensure research reproducibility and facilitate meaningful comparisons, concrete benchmarking experiments are crucial. These experiments involve running models against diverse prompt suites encompassing tasks like short dialogues, character continuity across scenes, object physics simulations, style transfer, and conditioning on reference images. Specific tests, such as those for character consistency using face embeddings and human ratings, or audio-visual sync tests with scripted dialogues and ASR alignment, are essential components of a thorough evaluation framework. Additionally, robustness tests using adversarial grammars are necessary to probe safety filters, requiring a commitment to ethical disclosure and a safe research environment.

The current landscape of text-to-video model evaluation faces several significant limitations. A primary challenge is the absence of a single, widely adopted benchmarking suite, which complicates consistent cross-model comparisons due to varied prompt designs and replicability issues. Generating coherent narratives that extend beyond a short duration (e.g., 15-30 seconds) remains a difficult task, highlighting a need for further research into scaling temporal modeling without compromising quality. Moreover, there is a clear demand for more grounded and controllable audio generation, including precise speaker identity and fine-grained lip sync, which necessitates improved multimodal alignment capabilities. Finally, the development of robust automatic detectors for critical safety concerns such as deepfakes, copyright infringement, and malicious prompts is still in its early stages, posing a significant challenge for effective safety and policy enforcement in this evolving domain.

## S1: Introduction to Magicboard Data Indexes

Your evaluation of text-to-video models needs a direct strategy. Combine automatic metrics and human assessments to truly understand performance.

Automatic metrics give you quantitative insights. For visual fidelity, FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) and LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity) assess individual frame quality. FVD (Fréchet Video Distance) measures temporal realism, which makes motion natural. CLIPScore or CLIP-Cosine quantifies text-video alignment, confirming content matches your input prompt. For dialogue, ASR-based lip-sync scores and audio-visual sync metrics confirm sound and visual synchronization. Action recognition consistency measures classifier agreement on actions across generated frames. Use datasets UCF101 and Kinetics for action consistency probes. DAVIS helps for object motion. Custom datasets support character and identity tests.

Human evaluations remain the gold standard for perceived quality. Use pairwise A/B tests and Likert ratings. These assess perceptual realism, narrative coherence, character consistency, and audio synchronization. Human assessments capture subtle errors and nuances automatic metrics miss.

For reproducible research, conduct concrete benchmarking experiments. Run per-model prompt suites across diverse domains. Measure FVD, CLIPScore, and LPIPS. Include prompts for short realistic dialogues, character continuity tasks, object physics like a bouncing ball, style transfer, and reference-image conditioning. Character consistency tests involve reference images and multi-scene prompts. They use face embeddings and human ratings. Audio-visual sync tests use scripted dialogues. ASR alignment and human ratings confirm lip-sync. Robustness tests probe safety filters with adversarial grammars. Ethical disclosure is required. Run these in a safe research environment.

Profile compute and latency. Measure per-second cost, per-frame latency, and GPU memory footprint for each model and tier.

The current landscape faces limitations. A single, agreed-upon benchmarking suite does not exist. This makes cross-model comparisons challenging due to varied prompt designs and replicability issues. Public leaderboards are needed to standardize benchmarking. Longer coherent narratives, beyond 15-30 seconds, remain difficult. Scaling temporal modeling without quality loss needs active research. Grounded, controllable audio generation, with speaker identity and fine-grained lip sync, needs better multimodal alignment. Robust automatic detectors for deepfakes, copyright, and malicious prompts remain immature. These are critical for safety and policy enforcement.
